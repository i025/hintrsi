## Observational visual audit

**Scope.** Image 1 is the original photograph, Image 2 the stage-5 initial render, and Image 3 the final delivered render. The observations below concern visible image appearance only; they do not establish hidden geometry, physical connections, code behavior, diagnosis, or adoption.

### Directly visible 2D observations

- **Object coverage:** Both renders depict the main visible furniture groups in Image 1: a central island, a tall glazed cabinet at the rear left, and upper and lower cabinetry along the rear right. The photograph also shows a partial doorway or cabinet edge at the far left and a wall switch that are not visible in either render. Image 1’s right-hand cabinet has a substantial dark side panel; that end is outside the frame in Images 2 and 3.
- **Position and direction:** In Image 1, the island top recedes toward the rear left, and its near end is prominent. In Images 2 and 3, the island reads more horizontally across the frame, with its long cabinet face prominent. The rear cabinet groups are recognizable but appear more separated and front-facing in the renders. Image 3 frames the island larger than Image 2.
- **Visible contacts:** In both renders, the island top visually meets its base, and the rear countertop visually meets the lower cabinets. The left upper cabinet also appears to sit on its lower section. These image contacts do not prove that the modeled parts are physically connected. The upper-right cabinets are visually separated from the countertop by a light-colored opening, with a visible vertical support at its left edge; Image 1 likewise shows an open backsplash area beneath those cabinets.
- **Components and detail:** The renders include framed doors, drawer fronts, handles, and glazing-like pale panels. Compared with Image 1, the panels in Images 2 and 3 look more uniformly opaque and pale, without the photograph’s pronounced reflections and varied transparency. The rendered island face has a regular repeated arrangement; the photograph shows a different visible arrangement of doors and hardware from its camera angle.
- **Material and texture:** Image 1 has darker, varied wood grain on the cabinetry, a golden island top with conspicuous grain, and irregular color variation in the floorboards. Images 2 and 3 have lighter, more uniform surfaces; their floor and island top show fine, regular linear patterns. The rendered cabinet wood does retain visible grain and color contrast with the top.
- **Lighting:** Image 1 has uneven natural-looking highlights and darker cabinet recesses. The renders are brighter and more evenly lit, with soft shadows under and behind the island. Images 2 and 3 also show visible grainy render noise, particularly around shadowed cabinet edges.
- **Camera and borders:** The photograph fills its frame with the room and furniture. Both renders show a conspicuous black area at the left where the light wall and floor end in the image. Image 2 crops the right cabinet run and places more empty wall above the furniture; Image 3 is a closer view but still crops the right-hand run and retains the black left border.

### Possible 3D or implementation interpretations

The black region could reflect limited room surfaces, background, or camera framing; the images alone do not identify which. The change in apparent island proportions and cabinet orientation could arise from camera placement, projection, geometry, or a combination. Occluded parts and actual object connectivity cannot be checked from these views. The differences between Images 2 and 3 show a changed visible composition, but do not by themselves establish what was changed in the scene or why.
