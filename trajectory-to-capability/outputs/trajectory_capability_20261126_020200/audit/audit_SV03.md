## Limited visual audit

The images show a broadly similar arrangement: a central wood-topped island, cabinetry along the rear walls, and a wood floor. The following comparisons concern visible appearance, not the correctness of hidden geometry.

### Directly visible 2D observations

- **Cabinet color and surface character:** The cabinetry in the photograph is dark, varied brown wood with visible grain and shading (Image 1). Both renders use lighter, more uniform brown cabinetry (Images 2 and 3). The island top is also paler and more even in the renders than in the photograph.
- **Upper cabinet details:** The photographed upper cabinets have comparatively substantial dark wood frames and glazing with reflections and horizontal divisions (Image 1). The rendered glazing looks more opaque and uniformly pale, while black marks at frame intersections and edges are conspicuous (Images 2 and 3). These marks are particularly prominent across the upper cabinets in both renders.
- **Rear cabinet arrangement:** In the photograph, the right-hand upper cabinet sits above an open backsplash and dark counter, with relatively few broad framed sections visible (Image 1). Image 2 depicts tall, narrow upper sections without an obvious middle horizontal division; Image 3 depicts a wider run of narrow sections with a prominent middle rail. Neither visible pattern closely matches every division in the photograph.
- **Island proportions and detailing:** The photographed island has a thick overhanging top and a dark base with recessed panels, drawers, doors and visible hardware (Image 1). Both renders show these broad features, but their island fronts have more regularly repeated, sharply outlined panels and conspicuous light or dark hardware marks (Images 2 and 3). The photographed base appears less uniformly segmented.
- **Floor and surroundings:** The photographed floor has varied narrow boards and noticeable wear or small dark marks (Image 1). The rendered floors look cleaner and more regular, with more conspicuous plank outlines (Images 2 and 3). The right side of each render also exposes a large plain wall area not presented in the same way in the photograph; the framing and viewpoint differ, so this is not a direct inventory of the room.
- **Viewpoint and composition:** The island occupies the foreground in all three images, but its apparent placement and the amount of surrounding cabinetry differ. Image 3 shows a long rear cabinet run behind the island, while Image 2 shows a shorter run and more empty wall to the right. Those differences are visible in the pictures, although perspective and framing affect the comparison.

### Geometry or implementation hypotheses—not established by the images

The dark marks at cabinet-frame junctions in Images 2 and 3 could reflect modeled hardware, intersections, materials, or rendering artifacts; the images alone do not establish which. Similarly, apparent panel borders, overhangs and cabinet alignment suggest possible differences in modeled proportions or construction, but do not prove physical connectivity or reveal geometry behind occlusion. The images provide no direct evidence of the underlying code, a teacher’s diagnosis, or what guidance either student adopted.

Images 2 and 3 are separate, history-dependent R2 generations, not a common-state intervention. Their visible differences do not establish a causal M1 effect, a universal quality ranking, or a conclusion about other experiments.
