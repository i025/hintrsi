[SceneEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37046) optimizes a reusable harness—tools, skills, middleware and memory—not model weights. A teacher reviews frozen-student programs, renders and traces; the archive contains both successful and unsuccessful candidates. This overlaps with trajectory distillation and teacher guidance, but specifies no paired-trajectory contrast or functional-state extraction (§3.3, lines 00124–00136, 00142).

SceneAct links image entities to a scene–asset–part hierarchy; spatial operators resolve relations against current geometry. The final harness inspects renders against both the reference and a JSON plan, with spatial checks and targeted repairs. This overlaps with program-state alignment, but does not specify alignment between trajectory states (Section 3.2, lines 00119–00123; Appendix D.2, lines 00785–00788).

Candidate harnesses are tested under identical budgets; the best replaces the incumbent only on a strictly higher development score combining SSIM, LPIPS and DINOv2. This is end-to-end selection of potentially reusable skills, not separate skill verification (§3.3, lines 00128–00131, 00137–00142).

Authors report teacher-free deployment and transfer to an unseen dataset and another model (§4.1, lines 00446–00450, 00455–00456). Search is costly, and development-score improvement need not generalize (§3.3, line 00142; Appendix E, line 00790). We did not replicate these claims; the planned fixed-evidence comparison remains distinct.
