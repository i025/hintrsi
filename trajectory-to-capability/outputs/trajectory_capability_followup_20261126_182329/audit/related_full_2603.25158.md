[Trace2Skill](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25158) distills success and failure trajectories into a human-readable skill directory, not model weights: clear overlap with trajectory distillation and fixed-model adaptation (§§2.1–2.3, lines 00120–00140). Analysts provide teacher-like guidance, although the same model ordinarily generates, analyzes and merges traces, without an external teacher (§2.4, lines 00144–00150).

Separate success and failure analysts extract lessons independently; merging favors recurrent, semantically similar patches, rather than explicitly pairing contrasting trajectories. Error analysts inspect artifacts and validate fixes against ground truth; success analysis is single-pass (§§2.3–2.4, lines 00135–00153; §I.2, lines 01640–01673). This overlaps with skill verification and program-state reasoning, but specifies no cross-trajectory functional-state alignment. Default consolidation applies all patches with edit-format guardrails; experimental subset selection scores candidate skills, at substantial validation cost (§§2.4, 4.3, lines 00148–00153, 00585–00597).

The authors report held-out and cross-model transfer, with some regressions; no separate tool-call budget was imposed (§§3.1–3.3, lines 00363–00370, 00379–00386; Table 1, lines 00324–00353; Appendix A, lines 01163–01169). Their sequential comparison does not isolate functional-state-aligned versus chronological extraction under fixed evidence and budgets (§4.1, lines 00497–00500, 00535–00541). We did not replicate these results.
