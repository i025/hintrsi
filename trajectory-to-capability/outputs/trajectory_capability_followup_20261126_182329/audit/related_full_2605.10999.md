[SkillGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10999) optimizes a single readable inference-time skill, optionally including scripts and references, without updating model weights (§§1–2, lines 00068–00070, 00084–00094).

- **Overlap and alignment:** It distills successful and failed base-agent trajectories; auxiliary LLMs provide teacher-like guidance, though not a separate teacher trajectory (§§3.1–3.2, lines 00104–00108, 00115–00119; §C.3, 00984–00985). It retrieves a nearest success using embeddings of input–summary pairs, checks task type, and contrasts full trajectories. This is semantic behavioral matching, not specified program-state alignment (§3.2, 00122–00126, 00138–00147).
- **Verification:** Paired outcomes on the same verification inputs count repairs minus regressions. Feedback refines candidates; best-of-K selection and a positive-gain gate determine deployment (§3.3, 00170–00185, 00189–00212).
- **Independence and limits:** Test instances are disjoint from construction, but verification data guide refinement and selection (§4, 00217–00221). Authors report frequent, not universal, cross-model transfer (§4, 00581–00585). Accepted skills can regress; construction incurs substantial token costs (§C.9, 01051–01054; §C.5, 01019–01029).

The paper does not test functional-state-aligned versus chronological extraction under fixed evidence and budgets. Its results are author-reported; we did not replicate them.
