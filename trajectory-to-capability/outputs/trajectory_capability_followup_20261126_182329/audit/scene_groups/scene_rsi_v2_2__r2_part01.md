## Comparison and interpretation

**Execution outcomes differ from reconstruction outcomes.** S0003, S0005 and S0006 recovered from a recorded initialization failure to a selected render. S0007–S0012 each had two technically successful renders and an executed revision. These are successful *operations*, not independently established successful scene matches. S0002 passed its first recorded error but failed at a later stage, a partial progression through execution rather than a partial-correctness score. S0001 and S0004 were interrupted by timeouts; their empty output does not identify the cause. No independently assessed visual success, partial-correctness finding, or formal quality score is supplied.

The attempts above contain substantive programs; they are not empty patches. Nor do the reported stops establish permission, branch or CLI-denial failures. Zero tool calls inside student CLI sessions are distinct from the runner executions recorded in the trajectories. A timeout is also distinct from a demonstrated algorithmic defect: proposed explanations involving scene complexity in S0001 and S0004 remain unverified. Conversely, the explicit fillet-bound and missing-world exceptions are observed technical errors, not proof that the complete reconstruction algorithm would otherwise succeed.

**Feedback and teacher roles must be separated.** Across these student trajectories, the supplied reports identify no distinct teacher diagnosis, proposed update or teacher-directed execution, and no evidence that such advice was received or adopted. They do not establish the contents of S0005’s unavailable later prompt. Runner tracebacks were actually received and used in revisions in S0001–S0003, S0005 and S0006; these are execution feedback, not teacher advice. In S0007–S0012, the runner supplied a first render for review, the students stated their own diagnoses, and revised programs were executed. Selection and `DONE` record acceptance or completion in the workflow, not independent confirmation of quality. S0006 is a counterexample to treating every returned revision as executed: its last proposal was not visibly run or selected.

**Transferable constraints and boundaries.** Constructor dimensional bounds and assumptions about Blender world state can prevent rendering; correcting a reported exception still requires a successful rerun. Rendering, selection, named asset hierarchy and stated part relationships do not alone verify resemblance, physical contact, pose, camera, lighting or material appearance. S0007–S0012 show that render feedback can be followed by concrete revisions, but their simultaneous changes prevent attribution of any improvement to a particular change. The specific layouts, fillet values, camera settings and object adjustments are task-specific. Student diagnoses—including S0009’s material-node explanation and S0012’s camera-match claim—remain hypotheses without independent image or geometric inspection. The paired identifiers and differing recorded outcomes do not establish causal superiority of either condition.
