## Cross-case findings

**Successful execution is narrower than successful reconstruction.** Many active trajectories contain multi-object programs, saved renders and selections. For example, S0101's `results/r2/generic_rsi/C2/selection/commons_133800864/` lines 3–5, 9–11, 15–17 and 19–22 record three successful executions and final selection; S0103's `results/r2/generic_rsi/C2/development/commons_118588033/` lines 3–22 similarly record three builds and selection. These are counterexamples to a claim that the workflow uniformly stopped. Conversely, a successful render, primitive inventory, `SELECT`, or `DONE` does not establish image resemblance, material appearance, correct pose, occlusion or physical contact. Grouped parts and coordinates establish intended structure, not that meshes actually meet. S0108's `results/r2/scene_rsi_pilot/C2/selection/commons_138420790/` lines 15–17 even specify a hood top above the ceiling deck while rendering successfully; the visual significance remains unverified.

**Failures have distinct boundaries.** CLI exit before a response, Blender exceptions in nonempty programs, and render timeouts are different observations. S0129's fillet and null-world tracebacks identify concrete execution problems; S0130's final repair lacks validation; S0043–S0050 and S0178–S0185 provide no stderr contents from which to diagnose the CLI exit. No cited example establishes a permission or branch stop, or a formally empty patch. Nor does an infrastructure stop demonstrate poor reconstruction behavior. Successful earlier artifacts in S0102 and S0110 show that a timeout need not erase prior operational progress.

**Advice, diagnosis and adoption must not be conflated.** Shared system procedures and controller requests were actually supplied to students, and renders were provided for review. The visible Scene trajectories do **not** identify a separate teacher message, teacher diagnosis or teacher-authored update; therefore teacher-advice availability, receipt and adoption cannot be established from them. S0112 and S0113 instead show students diagnosing and partially or fully implementing their *own* proposals after prompting. Runner tracebacks were also received and acted on in S0125 and S0129, but they are execution feedback, not teacher advice. Subsequent successful execution validates that code ran, not the diagnosis or visual improvement. Zero student-session tool calls do not negate separately recorded controller executions or expose hidden reasoning.

**Transferable constraints versus hypotheses.** The records support keeping execution status, selected artifact and independent quality assessment separate, and tracking whether a proposed change was actually run. They also show that a traceback can identify a concrete API or environment-state boundary, while an empty timeout log cannot establish its cause. A counterexample to overgeneralizing isolated interventions is S0100 lines 9–17: camera, geometry, palette and lighting changed together, so any visual effect cannot be attributed to one change. S0109, `results/r2/scene_rsi_pilot/C2/selection/commons_133800864/` lines 9–18, instead records successive camera revisions retaining the scene family and lighting; even there, the student's window-plane diagnosis and any improvement remain unverified without the images.

The students' kitchen, chaise, shipboard and workshop labels, wall-occlusion explanations, camera estimates and material corrections are **task-specific interpretations or hypotheses**, not independently established properties of the photographs. Across this batch, missing pixels, missing formal scores and absent geometric checks leave visual and contact quality unknown. These records support no causal superiority claim among conditions, and this synthesis is audit-only.
