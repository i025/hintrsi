## Comparison and interpretation

**Technical success versus failure.** Seven records show two successful render executions and selection of the second; S0273 shows an initial executable API failure followed by a successful retry. The first S0269 audit chunk alone could not establish the retry result, but its second chunk records successful rendering and selection. These are observations about execution, not formal judgments that a reconstruction matched its photograph. No record establishes an algorithmic failure of visual reconstruction, a partial visual-success grade, an empty patch, or a permission, branch or CLI stop. S0273’s traceback is a concrete code/API failure, not a visual diagnosis or an interruption caused by permissions. Zero recorded CLI tool calls in the relevant sessions likewise does not mean the SceneAct execution was absent.

**Advice and receipt.** The batch contains student trajectories, not a recorded teacher intervention. Across them, no separate teacher detection, diagnosis, proposed update, receipt or adoption is evidenced. Runner instructions and feedback must not be relabeled as teacher advice: for example, S0266 received a reference/render comparison request before its own diagnosis and revision (lines 7, 9–10), while S0273 actually received and acted on a traceback (lines 6, 8). Those establish receipt of runtime feedback and an executed response, not teacher involvement. No teacher-side availability record is supplied, so absence from these student traces does not prove advice was unavailable elsewhere.

**Diagnosis, update and validation.** The six successful comparison-and-revision examples plus S0269 and S0272 contain student-reported discrepancies after render input, not independently established defects. The updates were executed, and their resulting renders were selected, but no post-revision quality review or formal score is visible. S0273 is different: because its first execution produced no image, the feedback concerned the API error, and the student explicitly left appearance unverified. This is a counterexample to treating every second execution as a visually guided refinement.

**Transferable constraints and counterexamples.** A runnable, selected scene is not evidence of photographic fidelity, physical part contact, correct occlusion or material appearance. Code in these records declares multiple objects, child parts, poses, materials and camera/light settings, but the supplied audit text exposes no pixels for independent inspection. Conversely, lack of visual verification does not make the builds failures. Most revisions changed multiple parameter families simultaneously, preventing attribution of any outcome to one change. S0272 is a narrower counterexample: it retained geometry and appearance settings, but changed focal length and camera target together, so even there their separate effects are not isolated (lines 9–10).

The reported classroom wall, board and carpet discrepancies and the cafeteria glazing, lamp and palm discrepancies are **task-specific student hypotheses**, not general correction rules. S0268’s competing camera and room explanations and S0269’s competing camera and proportion explanations remain unresolved; neither successful rendering nor selection verifies them. The available evidence supports no causal superiority claim between conditions or revisions.
