## Transferable constraints and unresolved hypotheses

- **Separate execution, landmark and visual evidence.** Return codes and selection establish process outcomes; landmark tables expose numerical discrepancies; neither verifies visible contours, occlusion, material appearance, lighting or physical connections. S0323’s grouped child parts and S0322’s named objects are evidence of scene structure, not proof of correct attachment (S0323 lines 3–4; S0322 lines 3–4, 29).
- **Treat correspondences and causes as uncertain.** S0321’s post and S0323’s door-top discrepancies are specific measurements, not proof that camera settings alone caused a defect. S0322’s uncertain window correspondence is a counterexample to treating every projected-point difference as a confirmed framing error. Geometry, camera and reference estimates may all matter; the reports do not uniquely resolve them.
- **Keep scene-specific diagnoses separate from general constraints.** S0321’s larger cows and coarser coats, S0322’s roof and equipment changes, and S0323’s ceiling and floor changes were executed responses to H’s own comparisons. Whether those changes improved the selected images is unverified. S0323’s uncertain placement of the separately pictured GRID board illustrates that even the intended scene content can be ambiguous (line 3).

Across this batch, the evidence supports iterative, runner-mediated construction and technical rendering, with numerical checks and student-reported visual comparisons. It does not support a formal quality ranking, causal superiority claim, or independent conclusion that any selected scene matched its reference.
